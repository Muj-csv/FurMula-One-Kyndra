What's real, what's simulated

The cohort is simulated. Six animals, sized consistent with Shelter Animals Count's published per-organisation averages — not sourced from any individual shelter's records, and not depicting real animals.
The constraints are real. Powell et al. (2021, Scientific Reports), analysing 23,932 adoption records, found a 16.3% return rate for adopted dogs — 35% behavioural incompatibility, 18% conflict with an existing household pet. Every hard constraint in the matching engine maps to one of those two documented causes.
The compounding cost is real. Powell et al. (2022) followed returning adopters: only about 1 in 10 ever adopted again, and behavioural returners were four times less likely to than medical returners.
Why stability, not pure optimisation. Wu et al. (2025, JAMA Netw Open) found a mixed-integer optimiser beats Gale–Shapley on ophthalmology-residency data — 78.4% vs 70.9% matched to a top-3 choice. That's a real trade-off: optimisation can raise average fit, but Gale–Shapley guarantees no shelter–applicant pair would rather defect. For a placement a household might return, we chose the guarantee.
Kyndra proposes; it doesn't decide. Framed as odds, not outcomes: this reduces the odds staff overlook a bad pairing under time pressure, not "prevents returns." Human-baseline and 500-cohort randomized comparisons are still running ahead of Sept 13 — see the PRD for status.

Every constraint the engine enforces is inspectable from the results board. Run the cohort and open the details on any animal to see which households were eliminated, and on what documented grounds.
